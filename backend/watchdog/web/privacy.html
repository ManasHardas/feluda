Privacy summary

Neighborhood Watchdog is a private, invitation-only pilot run by your neighborhood organizer. This page says in plain words what the service handles. The full document is docs/PRIVACY.md in the public source repository.

What is collected

  • House number and street name, to match you to the list of addresses your organizer approved.
  • An e-mail address, used only to sign in with a one-time code.
  • Connectivity events from your phone: Wi-Fi lost or restored, internet reachable or not, cellular available or not, and when. Each event carries the phone platform, operating-system version and app version.
  • Optional: your internet provider and a label for your household.
  • A push-notification token so alerts can reach your phone.

What is never collected

  • Browsing history, websites, DNS lookups, messages or the contents of your phone.
  • Wi-Fi names, Wi-Fi passwords or router passwords. A scrambled fingerprint used to recognise your home network stays on your phone.
  • GPS or other location from your phone. The app does not ask for location permission.
  • Camera footage, microphone audio, or logins for cameras, alarms or internet providers.
  • Your legal name, or hardware identifiers of your phone.

Who can see what

  • You see your own household’s events and status.
  • Other residents see only anonymous totals, such as how many households were affected.
  • The organizer manages the address list and memberships. Organizers do not see e-mail addresses, device details or your event history.
  • Whoever operates the server and database can technically access stored data. Access is limited and logged.

How long it is kept

  • Connectivity events: 30 days. Incident summaries and audit records: 90 days. Account data: until you delete your account.
  • Leaving the neighborhood or deleting your account removes your devices and their events. Anonymous incident totals remain.

Your choices

  • Switch monitoring off at any time in the app. The app then reports that it is paused; it is never shown as healthy.
  • Leave the neighborhood or delete your account in the app’s settings.

Limits you should know

  • The app detects connectivity anomalies. It cannot confirm deliberate radio interference.
  • Phones, especially iPhones, pause apps in the background, so monitoring has gaps. No report does not mean no problem.
  • The service does not contact the police or anyone else on your behalf.
